12 Essential Strategies

01 Prepare First

Read through the sentences before the recording starts. This gives you context.

02 Predict Answer Type

Decide if the gap needs a Noun, Verb, or Number.

"Festivals are useful to..." (to + Verb) -> Predict: Preserve/Protect.
03 Use Grammar

The sentence must be grammatically correct. Check if "a/the" is needed or if a noun should be plural.

"are put aside" -> Requires plural noun (e.g., differences).
04 Order of Answers

Answers always appear in the same order as the recording. Follow Q1 -> Q2 -> Q3.

05 Word Limits

Strictly follow instructions (e.g., "NO MORE THAN ONE WORD"). Hyphenated words often count as one.

06 Keywords

Identify keywords before the gap to help locate the answer in the audio.

07 Paraphrasing

The speaker often uses different words with the same meaning. Listen for the meaning, not exact matches.

"Traced back" in question = "Originated" in audio.
08 Speed

You won't have much time. Practice spotting keywords and paraphrases quickly.

09 Location of Answer

Don't just wait for keywords. The answer might come before or after the keyword you spotted.

10-12 Final Checks

10. Guess! Never leave it empty.
11. Spelling counts.
12. Check your work.

Vocabulary

There are around 700,000 autistic people in the UK, and autism is a lifelong disability affecting communication, social interaction, and sensory experiences; although widely known, it is often misunderstood, which is why initiatives like Autism Hour—where businesses dim lights and reduce noise to prevent sensory overload that can cause stress or pain—are important in creating inclusive environments, expanding access for autistic people and their families, educating staff, and highlighting how sensory challenges also affect others who are not neuro-typical or who have conditions impacting their tolerance to light, noise, or other sensory input.
