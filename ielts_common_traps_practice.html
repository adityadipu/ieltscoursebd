Practice 1: A Busy School Day

Audio: recording19.mp3 | Write the number you hear

1. Arrived at minutes past seven.

2. students waiting, but only chairs.

3. Teacher said students would join, but only came.

4. Bell rang at past eight, not past.

5. Solved problems, not .

6. answers correct, but written on board.

7. students went outside, stayed inside.

8. Snacks cost dollars, not .

9. Lesson lasted minutes, not .

10. felt tired, had energy.

Yesterday, I arrived at school at sixteen minutes past seven. There were sixteen students waiting at the gate, but only sixty chairs in the hall. Our teacher said that thirty students would join us later, but only thirteen came on time. At fourteen minutes past eight, the bell rang, not at forty minutes past eight as some students thought. In the first class, we solved fifteen math problems, not fifty. The teacher reminded us that eighteen answers were correct, but eighty were written on the board by mistake. During the break, seventeen students went outside, while seventy stayed inside because it was cold. We bought snacks that cost nineteen dollars, not ninety. At twelve, we returned to class. Our final lesson lasted sixteen minutes, not sixty. By the end of the day, fourteen students felt tired, but forty said they still had energy.

Time: Past vs To

Digital Style

Five Twenty (5:20)

Four Forty (4:40)

"Past" (After hour)

Twenty past five (5:20)

Rule: Mins + Past + Hour

"To" (Before next hour)

Ten to two (1:50)

Quarter to eight (7:45)

Rule: Mins + To + Next Hour

Quick Practice: Write the digital time (e.g., 5:30)

Practice 3: City Learning Centre

Audio: recording20.mp3 | NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER

Woman: Good morning, City Learning Centre. How can I help you? Man: Hello, I’m calling to ask about your short courses. Woman: Of course. Which course are you interested in? Man: The English listening skills course. Woman: Right. The course starts at ten to two in the afternoon on Mondays. Man: Ten to two… so that’s 1:50 pm, correct? Woman: Yes, that’s right. Man: And how long does the class last? Woman: It finishes at five twenty. Man: Okay. Are there any breaks? Woman: Yes, there’s a short break at quarter to eight in the evening class, but not in the afternoon one. Man: I see. What about registration? Woman: Registration opens at four forty on the first day. Man: Four forty… that’s twenty to five, right? Woman: Exactly. Man: And when is the speaking workshop? Woman: That begins at half past six on Wednesdays. Man: Half past six—6:30. Got it. Woman: Please arrive early, at least by twenty-five to three, for the placement test. Man: Twenty-five to three… so 2:35 pm. Woman: Correct.

8. Why is "Ms" used for Rebecca Turner?

Receptionist: Good morning. City Cultural Centre. How may I help you? Student: Hello, I’d like to register for the evening communication course. Receptionist: Certainly. May I take your full name, please? Student: Yes. It’s Rebecca Turner. Receptionist: Thank you. Is that Turner — T-U-R-N-E-R? Student: Yes, that’s correct. Receptionist: Thank you, Ms Turner. Receptionist: And who recommended this course to you? Student: Dr Andrew Collins. Receptionist: Dr Collins, yes. He works with us part-time. Receptionist: Could you provide an emergency contact name, please? Student: Yes. It’s Mr James Wilson. Receptionist: Wilson — W-I-L-S-O-N? Student: That’s right. Receptionist: Thank you. Receptionist: Excuse me, Sir, could you please wait near the reception desk? Visitor: Of course. Receptionist: Ms Turner, your main instructor will be Miss Emma Clark. Student: Okay. Receptionist: That’s Clark — C-L-A-R-K. Student: Got it. Receptionist: The course coordinator is Mrs Robinson, but you’ll only meet her next week. Student: That’s fine. Receptionist: And finally, we occasionally have guest talks by Lady Bennett, one of our patrons. Student: Oh, that sounds interesting. Receptionist: Yes. Please take a seat. Mr Smith will call your name shortly.

The charity is a grassroots project aimed at providing nationwide support for local communities. People are able to show their support by donating money or useful objects like bicycles, so if you wish to give something, please get in touch. Alternatively, you may be interested in getting involved in the practical side of our charity. We will be holding a fundraiser soon, and the more people involved, the better.
