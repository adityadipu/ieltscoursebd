Step 1: Introduction (The "Puzzle" & Summary)
Pro Tip: Write this paragraph LAST! It should contain two main parts:
  • The Puzzle (The Hook): Start with a real-world contradiction or knowledge gap. (e.g., "Bangladesh has a rising GDP, yet poverty remains high. This puzzle inspired me to study policy...")
  • Objective & 3-Line Summary: Clearly state who you are, what program/university you are applying to, and summarize your long-term goal.
Step 2: Experience (Connecting the Dots)
How does your past prepare you for this Master's? Connect these dots:
  • Academic/Research: Mention relevant courses, degrees, and specific research methodologies (e.g., Qualitative research, Data Analysis, FGDs).
  • Volunteer/Work: Mention specific organizations (e.g., Political groups, Active Citizens).
  • Skill Translation: Show how these gave you Hard Skills (policy building, analysis) and Soft Skills (leadership, presentation) needed for a Master's.
Step 3: Why This University & Professor?
Prove you have researched their department thoroughly:
  • Specific Strengths: Why are they better than your current/previous university? Mention specific research facilities.
  • Professor Alignment: Name a specific professor. Analyze their work (e.g., "Professor [Name]'s work on state-sponsored oppression...") and state how your research interests align with theirs.
Step 4: Specific Career Goals
Map out your exact timeline and explain how this Master's helps achieve it:
  • Short-Term Goal: Be highly specific. (e.g., "I want to leverage the thesis experience from this Master's to prepare for a PhD at the University of Alberta.")
  • Long-Term Goal (5-7 years): Where do you see yourself? (e.g., "I aim to return to my home country as a Professor at Dhaka University.")
Step 5: Conclusion & Expectations
End on a confident and concise note:
  • Reiterate your confidence in your preparation.
  • Express hope and expectation to contribute to their dynamic academic environment.
