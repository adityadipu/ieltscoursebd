Subject–Verb Agreement Ultimate Mastery

1. Basic Rule

Singular → verb + s | Plural → base verb

👉 Reason: English verbs change only for singular subjects in present tense.
He plays football.
They play football.

2. Compound Subjects

👉 Reason: “A and B” means more than one → plural. But if it is one idea or same person → singular.
Reading and writing are important.
Bread and butter is my breakfast.
The CEO and founder is speaking.

3. Indefinite Pronouns

👉 Reason: Words like everyone, someone refer to one person at a time → singular.
Everyone is happy.
Someone has called.

4. Ignore Extra Phrases

👉 Reason: Words after “along with / as well as” are NOT the real subject.
The teacher along with students is coming.

5. A number vs The number

👉 “A number” = many → plural 👉 “The number” = one total → singular
A number of students are absent.
The number of students is increasing.

6. Either / Neither

👉 Verb follows nearest subject (last noun).
Either the teacher or students are ready.
Neither of the answers is correct.

7. Percentage

👉 Verb depends on noun after “of”.
50% of water is polluted.
50% of students are present.

8. Uncountable Nouns

👉 These cannot be counted → always treated as singular.
Information is useful.
Advice is helpful.

9. Gerund

👉 “-ing” acts like one activity → singular.
Swimming is good.
Reading helps learning.

10. Collective Nouns

👉 A group acts as one unit → singular 👉 Members acting separately → plural
The team is winning.
The staff are arguing.

Plural but Singular

News, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Ethics, Statistics, Measles, Mumps, Diabetes, Linguistics, Athletics, Gymnastics, Electronics, Phonetics, Genetics, Headquarters, Billiards, Darts, Summons

👉 These are subjects, diseases, or fields → treated as one thing.
Mathematics is difficult.
The news is shocking.

Always Plural

Clothes, Scissors, Trousers, Jeans, Shoes, Staff, Cattle, People, Parents, Children, Barracks, Goods, Assets, Proceeds, Earrings, Customs, Funds, Riches, Beggars, Surroundings, Manners, Wings, Premises, Credentials, Scales, Amenities, Shorts, Athletics, Groceries

👉 These refer to pairs or groups → always plural.
The police are here.
My trousers are tight.

Collective Nouns

Team, Family, Group, Audience, Committee, Class, Crew, Staff, Crowd, Government, Organization, Panel, Population, Faculty, Herd, Fleet, Board, Tribe, Association, Council, Choir, Army, Congregation, Band, Company, Club, Jury, Delegation, Assembly, Chorus, Flock, Pack, Squad, Mob, Gang, Public, Union, Tribe

👉 These represent one group → singular verb (usually).
The team is winning.
The government has taken action.
