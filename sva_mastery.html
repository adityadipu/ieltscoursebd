Subject–Verb Agreement (SVA)

Module 9: Complete Mastery System

1. Basic Rule

Singular subject → verb + s (or is/has/was)
Plural subject → base verb (or are/have/were)

👉 Reason: English verbs change only for singular subjects in the present tense to show agreement.
Correct: He plays football.
Correct: They play football.

2. Compound Subjects (and)

👉 Reason: “A and B” means more than one, creating a plural subject. However, if the two nouns refer to the exact same person or a single unified concept, it is singular.
Plural: Reading and writing are important.
Singular (One concept): Bread and butter is my breakfast.
Singular (Same person): The CEO and founder is speaking.
Plural (Two people): The CEO and the manager are here.

3. Indefinite Pronouns

👉 Reason: Words like everyone, someone, nobody, anyone, each, either, neither refer to one person/thing at a time, so they are grammatically singular.
Correct: Everyone is happy.
Correct: Each student has a book.
Correct: Nobody knows the answer.

4. Ignore Extra Phrases

👉 Reason: Phrases separated by commas or starting with "along with", "as well as", "in addition to", "together with" are NOT part of the real subject. Ignore them to find the verb.
Correct: The teacher, along with his students, is coming.
Correct: The manager as well as the staff works hard.

5. A number vs. The number

👉 Reason: "A number" means "many" (Plural). "The number" refers to one specific total figure (Singular).
Plural: A number of students are absent.
Singular: The number of students is increasing.

6. Or / Nor (Proximity Rule)

👉 Reason: When subjects are joined by or, nor, either...or, neither...nor, the verb agrees with the subject CLOSEST to it.
Correct: Either the teacher or the students are ready. (Agrees with students)
Correct: Neither the students nor the teacher is ready. (Agrees with teacher)

7. Fractions and Percentages

👉 Reason: For fractions (half of, a third of) and percentages (50% of), the verb depends entirely on the noun that comes AFTER the word "of".
Singular: 50% of the water is polluted. (Water is uncountable/singular)
Plural: 50% of the students are present. (Students is plural)

8. Uncountable Nouns

👉 Reason: Things you cannot physically count (Information, advice, furniture, luggage, water) are always treated as singular.
Correct: The information is highly useful.
Correct: Her advice was helpful.

9. Gerunds (V+ing) as Subjects

👉 Reason: An "-ing" word acting as a noun represents a single activity or concept, taking a singular verb.
Correct: Swimming is excellent exercise.
Correct: Reading books helps your grammar.

10. Here and There

👉 Reason: "Here" and "There" are never the subject. The real subject comes AFTER the verb in these sentences.
Singular: There is a problem.
Plural: There are many problems.

1. Plural Form but Singular Meaning

List: News, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Ethics, Statistics, Measles, Mumps, Diabetes, Linguistics, Athletics, Gymnastics, Electronics, Phonetics, Genetics, Headquarters, Billiards, Darts, Summons

👉 Reason: These words end in "-s", making them look plural, but they refer to a single subject, disease, or field of study. Treat them as singular.
Mathematics is difficult.
The news is shocking.

2. Always Plural Nouns

Pairs List: Clothes, Scissors, Trousers, Jeans, Shoes, Shorts, Glasses, Binoculars.
Group List: Police, Staff, Cattle, People, Parents, Children, Barracks, Goods, Assets, Proceeds, Earrings, Customs, Funds, Riches, Beggars, Surroundings, Manners, Wings, Premises, Credentials, Scales, Amenities, Groceries.

👉 Reason: These nouns represent items with two parts (like scissors) or collective groups considered as multiples. They always take plural verbs.
The police are investigating the crime.
My trousers are tight.
The cattle are grazing.

3. Collective Nouns

List: Team, Family, Group, Audience, Committee, Class, Crew, Staff, Crowd, Government, Organization, Panel, Population, Faculty, Herd, Fleet, Board, Tribe, Association, Council, Choir, Army, Congregation, Band, Company, Club, Jury, Delegation, Assembly, Chorus, Flock, Pack, Squad, Mob, Gang, Public, Union.

👉 Reason: If the group acts as a single, unified body, use a SINGULAR verb. If the members of the group are acting individually/arguing, use a PLURAL verb. (In IELTS, singular is safest unless specified).
Unified (Singular): The team is winning the championship.
Divided (Plural): The jury are arguing among themselves.
